Quality isn't optional. It's not something you add at the end or trade off for speed. It's fundamental to building software that actually works.

## What is High-Quality Software?

High-quality software:

- **Works correctly** — It does what it's supposed to do
- **Is reliable** — It keeps working over time, under load, when things go wrong
- **Is maintainable** — Other developers (including future you) can understand and change it
- **Is efficient** — It uses resources reasonably

Notice that "meets requirements" isn't enough. Software can meet every written requirement and still be unreliable, unmaintainable garbage.

## The Cost of Low Quality

Low-quality software has real costs:

- **Bugs in production** — Users lose trust. In some domains, people get hurt.
- **Slow development** — Bad code is hard to change. Every feature takes longer.
- **Developer burnout** — Working in a messy codebase is demoralizing.
- **Business failure** — Competitors with better software win.

Put simply: only high-quality software survives in the market.

## Quality Enables Speed

There's a common misconception that quality and speed are opposites — that you can "move fast and break things."

In reality, quality enables sustainable speed:

- Clean code is faster to modify
- Good tests catch bugs before they reach production
- Well-designed systems are easier to extend
- Teams that maintain quality can keep delivering, month after month

Cutting quality feels faster in the short term. In the medium and long term, it slows everything down.

## Professional Software

We build software for people who pay for it — whether that's direct customers, employers, or product owners. These are our clients.

Professional software engineers deliver software that:

- Solves the client's problem
- Works reliably
- Can be maintained and extended

This is different from hobby projects or prototypes. Professional software needs to work, and it needs to keep working.
