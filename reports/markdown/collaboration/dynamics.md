Software is built by teams. How those teams communicate and work together often matters more than individual technical skill.

## What Makes Teams Effective

Effective teams share a few characteristics:

- **Shared understanding** — Everyone knows what they're building and why
- **Trust** — People believe their teammates are competent and have good intentions
- **Psychological safety** — People feel safe to ask questions, admit mistakes, and disagree
- **Clear communication** — Information flows freely; no one is left in the dark

These sound soft, but they directly impact delivery. A team with great communication and average skills will outperform a team with great skills and poor communication.

## How Information Flows

In healthy teams, information flows easily. People share what they're working on, what's blocking them, and what they've learned.

In unhealthy teams, information gets stuck:

- **Silos** — Teams or individuals hoard knowledge
- **Bottlenecks** — Everything routes through one person
- **Assumptions** — People think others know things they don't

Most communication problems aren't about tools. They're about habits and trust.

## Knowledge Sharing

Code doesn't explain itself. Teams need deliberate practices to share knowledge:

- **Code reviews** — Not just catching bugs, but spreading understanding
- **Pair programming** — Real-time knowledge transfer (covered more in Practices)
- **Documentation** — Writing things down so others (and future you) can understand
- **Conversations** — Sometimes the best documentation is a five-minute chat

The goal is to avoid single points of failure — situations where only one person understands something critical.

## Common Dysfunctions

Watch for these patterns:

- **Blame culture** — When something breaks, people look for who to blame instead of what to fix
- **Information hoarding** — Knowledge as power instead of knowledge as shared resource
- **Hero culture** — Celebrating people who work 80-hour weeks to "save" projects
- **Meeting overload** — Substituting meetings for actual communication

## Remote and Distributed Teams

Remote work amplifies both good and bad communication patterns.

What helps:

- **Over-communicate** — What's obvious to you isn't obvious to others
- **Write things down** — Async communication needs documentation
- **Be intentional about connection** — Casual hallway conversations don't happen automatically
- **Assume good intent** — Text lacks tone; give people the benefit of the doubt
