Agile is not a methodology. It's not Scrum, sprints, or stand-ups. It's a set of values.

## The Agile Manifesto

In 2001, seventeen software developers published four value statements:

> **Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools  
> **Working software** over comprehensive documentation  
> **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation  
> **Responding to change** over following a plan

The items on the right have value. But the items on the left have _more_ value.

## What This Means

**Individuals and interactions over processes and tools**  
Processes and tools help, but they don't replace people talking to each other. A team with great communication and mediocre tools will outperform a team with great tools and poor communication.

**Working software over comprehensive documentation**  
Documentation has its place, but the primary measure of progress is software that actually works. A 100-page spec means nothing if the software doesn't run.

**Customer collaboration over contract negotiation**  
Instead of hiding behind contracts and requirements documents, work _with_ the customer throughout. They're not adversaries — they're partners in solving the problem.

**Responding to change over following a plan**  
Plans are useful, but they're guesses about the future. When reality differs from the plan, adapt. Don't cling to a plan that no longer makes sense.

## The Twelve Principles

Behind the manifesto are twelve principles. A few key ones:

- Deliver working software frequently
- Welcome changing requirements, even late in development
- Build projects around motivated individuals and trust them
- The best architectures and designs emerge from self-organizing teams
- Working software is the primary measure of progress
- Sustainable pace — the team should be able to maintain this indefinitely

## Agile as Foundation

These values inform everything in this guide. When we discuss practices like TDD or pair programming, they exist because they embody these values — fast feedback, collaboration, working software, adaptation.
